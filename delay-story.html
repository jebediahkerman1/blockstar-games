MINE THEFT AUTO:
THE DELAY EXPLAINED.

The city is huge. The bugs are somehow bigger. Blockstar Games explains why its ambitious block-built crime sandbox is taking longer than planned.

MINE THEFT AUTO
COMING WHEN IT'S READY.

We know you've been asking the same question: “When is Mine Theft Auto coming out?” It's a fair question. The project has a big city, big plans, and a development process that occasionally seems to be held together with a crafting table and pure optimism.

So here's the official explanation from Blockstar Games: the game isn't abandoned. It's just busy trying to become a game instead of a collection of spectacular technical incidents.

SO, WHAT'S GOING WRONG?

  • NPCs have their own plans. Pedestrians don't always follow their paths. Some wander off, some stop in the middle of the road, and others behave like they've never seen a pavement before.
  • Vehicles are unpredictable. Traffic is meant to make the city feel alive. Right now, it can feel like every driver has accepted a side quest to cause chaos.
  • Los Santos occasionally forgets it's a city. Buildings and bits of infrastructure have a habit of disappearing, floating, or simply refusing to cooperate when the world loads.
  • The wanted system needs work. A proper crime sandbox needs police and consequences. The current plan is to make those systems fun and reliable before calling the experience finished.
  • The pavement is not always pavement. Testing has included the classic open-world experience of walking down a street and unexpectedly falling straight through it. Immersion is important; falling into the void is less important.
  • Performance and compatibility need testing. With a large map and a stack of mods working together, the team needs to make sure the experience is stable instead of assuming that a successful launch means everything is fine.
“We are aiming for a playable city, not a world-record speedrun of finding every bug.”

IS THE PROJECT ABANDONED?

No. Blockstar Games thanks everyone who's followed the project and confirms that development is still ongoing. The project files and GitHub presence are still there, and the goal remains the same: build a block-based open-world experience worth exploring.

Current testing includes the very important milestone of being able to walk around Los Santos without the road giving up and sending the player through the floor. It may not sound glamorous, but a city needs functioning ground before it can deliver high-speed chases.

WHEN WILL IT RELEASE?

There is no confirmed release date to announce right now. Rather than promise a date and then move it every time a traffic NPC walks into a wall, Blockstar Games will share more concrete milestones when they're ready.

The next steps are to improve stability, sort out the city and NPC behaviour, test the vehicles and wanted-system ideas, and keep checking how the full mod setup performs. The plan is to make progress in stages instead of pretending every feature is already finished.

THANKS FOR STICKING AROUND.

We appreciate everyone who's been waiting, testing, giving feedback, or just checking in to see whether the city has stopped eating its own roads. Mine Theft Auto is still a work in progress, and there's plenty left to do.

Until the next update: drive carefully, trust absolutely no pavement, and remember that “coming when it's ready” is not a date—even if we put it in very large letters.
